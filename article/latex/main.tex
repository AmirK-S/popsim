% === GENERATED:ABSTRACT BEGIN (auto -- regenerated by md2latex.py, do not hand-edit) ===
Published LLM ``digital twins'' --- per-respondent simulated answer vectors released alongside survey panels --- are linkable to the humans they were built from, and that risk has a trajectory. Measured on three datasets and three generations of models, the twins of 2023 did not beat a demographic baseline recomputed on their own pool, and the twins of today exceed theirs. On the GPT-3 twins Argyle et al. released in 2023, the strongest designates the right respondent among 2,148 in \textbf{0.14~\%} [0.05~;~0.27] of cases against 0.10~\% [0.02~;~0.23] for that baseline --- intervals that overlap, at a per-item accuracy \textit{below} the modal answer. On twins built with recent models the same attack reaches \textbf{20.7~\%} [19.0~;~22.4] on Twin-2K-500 (2,058 respondents) in a closed pool and \textbf{4.28~\%} [3.3~;~5.6] in an open world at 1~\% false accusations, against a human test-retest ceiling of 54.5~\%. \textbf{Twin-2K-500 is the dataset that carries the claim}: armed identically on the same basin, a demographics-only comparator sits \textbf{29.9$\times$} below the twin, and the gap survives giving a classical comparator the twin's own input. On the Park et al. archive the corresponding rates are \textbf{not evidence that the twin leaks beyond demographics}: that archive's ``demographics-only'' condition is conditioned on \textbf{eleven} attributes that leave almost every respondent unique in their own sample, and armed identically it comes within a ratio of \textbf{1.06$\times$} of the agent (\S\,5.8). \textbf{The three datasets differ in team, protocol, item count and pool, so ``the risk grew with model capability'' is confounded with ``the protocols differ''; we state that confound in front rather than resolve it.} Inside a shared pipeline we report a twin-to-twin channel: two twins of the same person designate each other with no real human answer held by the attacker --- at 60 common items, 30 ordered pairs of \textbf{six} configurations running from 11.9~\% to 83.6~\% top-1, mean 36.4~\% [24.7~;~56.5] on those six units. The ``distance law'' we preregistered --- leakage falling with the number of differing elements --- is refuted by our own measurements, and we claim no converse law; whether the channel survives between \textit{independently built} pipelines is untested. Our control is not new --- it is the pre-spend, gating form of Anonymeter's control baseline --- and it costs nothing: no experimental arm is interpretable until its candidate pipeline beats a demographic baseline against the real humans. It stopped two of our own paid experiments and the Argyle replication. We also report what we could not establish. We preregistered that the observed coupling between imitation quality and leakage reflects an individual fingerprint; a null carrying only a matched per-person accuracy margin --- which in fact leaks more than our own twin (31.6~\% against 20.7~\%) --- reaches a rank correlation as high as ours (0.980, 5th--95th percentiles [0.951~;~0.993], against 0.965 observed). Of the thirteen refutations in our table of preregistered predictions, none third-party timestamped, nine rest on a plan git history shows committed before its first result, three on a plan committed with its results, and one refutes a subgroup defined after them; one prediction is withdrawn, two are inconclusive and one untestable; our own twins failed to reproduce the leakage even with a paid frontier model; and the mechanism concentrating the effect on one item block stays open, though six candidate explanations are now excluded. \textbf{The headline rate also rests on a single generation that is not ours and whose parameters its authors have not published}: across seven configurations 99.07~\% of the observed variability is attributable to the generation configuration and 0.93~\% to the people, so it must be read as the rate of the most faithful configuration we hold, not of an LLM twin in general. Finally, permuting a twin's answers within a demographic segment does not protect against an adaptive attacker, whereas aggregating a segment's twins by groups of k bounds top-1 by 1/k against any attacker, at a cost we state (\S\,6); we withdraw our preregistered comparison with differential privacy.

\section{Introduction}

Survey research is beginning to publish \textit{digital twins}: for each human respondent in a panel, an LLM is conditioned on that person's profile and its simulated answers to the questionnaire are released as a per-person record. Toubia et al. published 2,058 such twins as Twin-2K-500 \cite{toubia2025twin2k500}. Park et al. built generative agents for 1,052 participants and cited privacy in restricting access to individual agent responses \cite{park2024agents} --- although the publicly posted replication package does carry those individual responses together with the agent conditions we analyse (\S\,4.1, \ethicsname{}). The privacy question these releases raise has been named --- by Park et al. themselves, by Bonagiri et al. \cite{bonagiri2026cognitive}, and by the AAPOR task force \cite{aapor2026responsibleai}, which ranks synthetic response generation as the most risky of the core tasks it evaluates and names re-identification by linkage without quantifying it --- but it has not been measured.

This paper measures it on three datasets spanning three generations of models, and reports both what the measurement supports and what it refutes.

\subsection{The trajectory, and the confound that comes with it}

Argyle et al. published in 2023 GPT-3 twins of ANES respondents, 12 matched items per person \cite{argyle2023outofone}. Attacked exactly as we attack the recent panels, those twins do not demonstrably beat a demographic baseline recomputed on their own pool of 2,148: the intervals overlap, at a per-item accuracy \textit{below} the modal answer, and a nearest neighbour given the same eleven true answers carries \textbf{3.6 times more identity bits} (\S\,5.8). Two years later, twins of the same kind of panel are re-identified at 20.7~\% in a closed pool of 2,058 and 4.28~\% in an open world at 1~\% false accusations --- and 29.9 times their identically armed demographic comparator (\S\,5.8). The risk was not measurable in the first published generation of twins; it is substantial in the current one.

\textbf{The confound, stated before the claim rather than after it.} The three datasets differ in team, protocol, item count (12 against 60 and 177), pool and questionnaire. ``The risk grew with model capability'' is therefore confounded with ``the protocols differ''. At matched \textbf{effective} information --- the same independent item count on both sides, the same pool size, the same attack and the same tie-breaking convention --- the twins of 2025 exceed their own demographic baseline while none of the three twins of 2023 exceeds theirs. Matching on the raw number of items is not enough: twelve Twin items still carry 46~\% more independent information than the twelve ANES items, and the number of items inflates every attacker, the demographic baseline included, by a factor of 7.4 between 12 and 60 items. We therefore report a \textbf{direction, not a magnitude}: the 2023 endpoint fails our interpretability control and is not estimable, its ratio to baseline wandering between 1.4 and 3.7 across subsamples of its own pool, and relative to a nearest-neighbour attacker holding the same items the two ends differ by less than we can separate from noise. Nor are they in one metric: closed-world top-1 under a naive Hamming attack at the left, true detections at 1~\% false accusations under A-LLR at the right, the second \textbf{undefined} at the left end, where its open-world AUC is indistinguishable from zero. These witnesses are post-hoc, and the matched-information figures are in the appendix. Lifting the confound would need the same items, the same population and several generations of models with individually matched twins; no such resource is public (\S\,7.4). We state the trajectory as a measured contrast between three published corpora, never as a causal effect of model capability.

One reading must be excluded. The Argyle result is \textbf{not a refutation of the scale trend we report}: it is out of domain. That trend is measured on twins that carry a person (\S\,5.9), and our interpretability control (\S\,4.5) establishes that these twins carry none we can demonstrate. Taking 0.14~\% for a refutation would commit, in reverse, the exact error the control exists to prevent --- concluding from a measurement taken on noise (\S\,5.8).

\subsection{What we set out to show, and what happened}

Our central hypothesis was that a twin's fidelity to \textit{its particular person} and that twin's identifiability are expressions of one underlying axis. We preregistered a self-refutation test in two parts. The first passed: the rank correlation between imitation quality and leakage survives measuring the two axes on completely disjoint item sets (Spearman 0.969, 95~\% CI [0.937~;~0.993], 50 stratified random A/B splits of 60 items). The relation is not an artifact of recycled computation.

The second part failed, and against us. We constructed a \textit{marginal null}: 100 artificial predictors matched to each person's own accuracy margin, with correct positions drawn at random without looking at the person --- an object that is not structure-free, and that in fact leaks more than our own twin (31.6~\% top-1 against 20.7~\%). Adversarial review found a real defect in that first witness, since rebuilt without changing the verdict (\S\,5.1). The rebuilt witness reaches mean Spearman \textbf{0.980}, 5th--95th percentiles [0.951~;~0.993], against \textbf{0.965} observed over the 12 configurations; the observed value does not exceed it. Each null is compared only against the observed value measured on its own plan. \textbf{Reproducing the coupling requires nothing more than a matched per-person accuracy and correct positions drawn at random --- prediction (b) is refuted.}

We report this as a result rather than a confession. A recent line of work argues that an attack on foundation models proves nothing until the null hypothesis is properly sampled: blind baselines routinely beat state-of-the-art membership inference attacks \cite{das2024blind,duan2024membership}, and a SaTML position paper makes the demand explicit \cite{zhang2024satml}. The quality-leakage coupling reported in prose by Ward et al. \cite{ward2025synthmia} and Byun et al. \cite{byun2025riskcontext}, and contested by Platzer and Reutterer \cite{platzer2021holdout} and Adams et al. \cite{adams2025fidelity}, has never been tested against such a control. We built it, and it absorbs our own effect. What survives is not a theory of why twins leak, but measurements that do not depend on the coupling being individual-specific at all.

\subsection{Contributions}

\textbf{(1) A trajectory measured across three generations of published twins.} The same attack, under the same control, on twins released in 2023, 2024 and 2025: at their own demographic baseline then, 29.9$\times$ above it now in the closed pool (\S\,5.8), with the protocol confound of \S\,1.1 forbidding any reading of it as an effect of model capability alone.

\textbf{(2) A pre-spend stop rule, and what it cost us.} The discipline is not ours: Anonymeter builds a control baseline into its risk estimate \cite{giomi2023unified}, and blind baselines demand the same of membership attacks \cite{das2024blind,zhang2024satml}. What is ours is the gating form, consuming no model call and applied before the first paid arm: \textbf{no experimental arm is interpretable until its candidate pipeline has been shown to beat the demographic baseline in top-1 against the real human answers} (\S\,4.5). It stopped two of our own paid arms (\S\,5.4), the Argyle replication (\S\,5.8), and the \texttt{persona} condition of the Park archive, where an agent built on a person's own self-description re-identifies her \textit{less} well than demographics alone.

\textbf{(3) A twin-to-twin linkage channel within a shared pipeline, whose rate is not governed by how many pipeline elements differ.} Two twins of the same person, sharing their persona source and differing only in model or output format, designate each other while the attacker holds no real human answer of any kind, on both Twin-2K-500 and the Park archive and against anti-artifact controls at or below 0.31~\%; no number for this channel is reported without its common-item count. At one pipeline element changed the rate runs from 17.2~\% to 81.4~\%, and the \textit{distance law} we preregistered --- leakage falling with the number of differing components --- is refuted by our own measurements (\S\,5.4). Anonymeter's low linkability figure is not a counter-result, for the reason given in \S\,2.1. Whether the channel crosses \textit{independently built} pipelines is untested: the arm meant to decide it is not interpretable (\S\,5.4).

\textbf{(4) The leakage is localised in the pattern of answers, not in memorised content}, by an ablation that permutes the order of a twin's answers while leaving their content untouched (\S\,5.5): what identifies is the dependence structure between answers. The pipeline trains nothing on the target population, so no train/test gap exists to exploit, unlike Yeom et al. \cite{yeom2018privacy} and Feldman \cite{feldman2020memorization}; but our training-cutoff control lives in a regime where none of our twins identifies almost anyone (\S\,5.7), so \textbf{we do not claim a measured dissociation between memorization and re-identification.} That narrower claim is what separates our channel from the memorization \cite{carlini2021extracting} and free-text inference literatures \cite{staab2024beyond,ko2026weakcues,lermen2026deanonymization}. What concentrates the effect on one item block stays open, but six candidate explanations are now excluded (\S\,5.5).

\textbf{(5) The Narayanan--Shmatikov attack, instantiated on twins, with its human ceiling.} Both moves are theirs \cite{narayanan2008robust}: the rarity-weighted likelihood score, and the out-of-sample criterion that reports true detections against false accusations (\S\,5.3, Figure 1). New here are the object, the pool, the human ceiling, and out-of-fold estimation of the weights. It corrects our own published Park figure upward --- and raises that archive's demographic comparator further still, which is why Park no longer carries this paper's claim (\S\,5.8).

\textbf{(6) Consequences for publication, developed in a companion paper.} Permutation does not protect, even in its most favourable case; within-segment aggregation by the mode bounds top-1 by 1/k against any attacker, at a cost in between-segment statistics that we publish beside it; our variant of the 1998 Post Randomisation Method carries \textbf{no formal guarantee}; and we withdraw our preregistered comparison with differential privacy (\S\,6).

\textbf{(7) A bits-of-identity instrument that transports across datasets where the raw rate does not.} A derived instrument, not a discovery: a case of Rényi min-entropy leakage \cite{smith2009foundations}, in the spirit of Eckersley's surprisal \cite{eckersley2010unique}, addressing the transportability problem of the Rocher et al. scaling law \cite{rocher2025scaling} by another route (\S\,5.6).

\subsection{Negative results, stated in front}

Table~\ref{tab:predictions} lists thirteen refuted predictions, one withdrawn, two inconclusive and one that could not be tested at all (\S\,7.1); only nine of the thirteen refutations rest on a preregistration that git history shows committed before the first result, none is third-party timestamped, and one (row 4) refutes a subgroup defined after the results; three later verdicts --- the distance law (\S\,5.4) and the third dataset's two predictions (\S\,5.8) --- sit outside that table for the reasons given there. Our own twins identify almost nobody, and the constructive demonstration meant to close that gap --- raise fidelity with a frontier model and watch the leakage return --- \textbf{failed}: \texttt{openai/\allowbreak{}gpt-\allowbreak{}4\allowbreak{}.1} on Twin's per-item recipe left fidelity at 0.1714 against their 0.708 (\S\,5.7). We still do not know what the Twin-2K-500 recipe does differently. The coupling that motivated this work does not survive its own control (\S\,5.1). And \textbf{the headline rate is the rate of one generation of one configuration} --- the most faithful of the eight we measured, and also the highest --- which we did not produce and cannot re-run, its authors not having published the parameters behind it (\S\,7.4). We consider a reader who stops here to have read the paper honestly.

\section{Background and Related Work}

\subsection{Privacy of synthetic data}

Stadler, Oprisanu and Troncoso show that generated tabular data remains linkable to its sources \cite{stadler2022groundhog}. Giomi et al. formalise three attacks --- singling out, linkability, inference --- and conclude, on their datasets, that linkability is the weakest risk \cite{giomi2023unified}. That conclusion is contested: Annamalai, Gadotti and Rocher \cite{annamalai2024linear} and Ganev and De Cristofaro \cite{ganev2025inadequacy,ganev2026rethinking,ganev2024regulatory} show reconstruction attacks defeating distance-to-closest-record metrics; Yao et al. \cite{yao2025dcr} and Meeus et al. \cite{meeus2023achilles} generalise the critique; Golob, Pentyala and De Cock make these attacks an emerging standard \cite{golob2026sok}. Houssiau et al.'s TAPAS formalises attacker knowledge and the baselines an audit must beat \cite{houssiau2022tapas}.

\textit{What distinguishes us.} That corpus is tabular microdata from classical generators, judged by continuous similarity metrics. Ours is a categorical answer vector produced by an LLM conditioned on a person, where no similarity metric replaces a direct matching attack.

\subsection{Re-identification of real data}

Narayanan and Shmatikov re-identify Netflix users \cite{narayanan2008robust}; de Montjoye et al. show four spatio-temporal points suffice on credit-card metadata \cite{demontjoye2015unique}; Rocher, Hendrickx and de Montjoye model risk on incomplete samples \cite{rocher2019estimating} and extend it into a scaling law \cite{rocher2025scaling}. Taub et al. ground the statistical measurement of disclosure risk on synthetic data (CAP, TCAP) on the premise that synthetic generation breaks the link between identity and datum \cite{taub2018differential}.

\textit{What distinguishes us.} These works re-identify from genuine auxiliary data. Our attack's input is never a real datum of the target but a model output generated from a persona, matched against real answers the attacker separately holds. That falsifies the CAP/TCAP premise, on LLM-simulated survey microdata.

\subsection{Privacy and LLMs}

Carlini et al. document memorization and regurgitation of training sequences \cite{carlini2021extracting}, and show memorization growing near log-linearly with model scale \cite{carlini2023quantifying} --- the leakage-versus-capability precedent closest to our own trajectory. Staab et al. infer personal attributes from innocuous free text \cite{staab2024beyond}. Ko et al. de-anonymise online authors by multi-cue agentic inference \cite{ko2026weakcues} --- the closest threatening neighbour --- and Lermen et al. demonstrate the same attack at scale \cite{lermen2026deanonymization}. Theoretically, Yeom et al. connect membership attacks to overfitting \cite{yeom2018privacy}; Feldman shows memorizing rare cases is necessary for generalization \cite{feldman2020memorization}; tracing codes \cite{bun2014fingerprinting,dwork2015robust} are the closest formal parent to our 1-in-N attack, bounding the worst case of an optimal adversary recovering an individual from aggregate statistics, though in dimension far exceeding the number of individuals.

\textit{What distinguishes us.} Those works all start from free text bearing direct semantic cues, aggregated by an agent reasoning over it. Our channel is narrower and drier: closed-choice categorical answers, no free text, no agentic reasoning, a mechanism localised in the dependence structure rather than in memorised content (\S\,5.5). Unlike Yeom and Feldman, our pipeline trains no model on the target population.

\subsection{Respondent simulation and twins}

Argyle et al. launched the use of LLMs as panel substitutes \cite{argyle2023outofone}; their Dataverse archive supplies the 2023 end of our trajectory, and our measurement does not contradict their paper, whose study 3 compares matrices of Cramér's V --- the association structure of a sample, not individual accuracy. Park et al. cite privacy in restricting access to their 1,052 agents' individual responses \cite{park2024agents}, while the publicly posted replication package nonetheless carries individual participant and agent responses (\ethicsname{}) --- a risk anticipated, never measured. Toubia et al. publish the 2,058-twin panel we attack, treating neither privacy nor linkage \cite{toubia2025twin2k500}. Peng et al. document group-fidelity distortions on that same panel without connection to re-identification \cite{peng2026funhouse}. Bonagiri et al. issue a normative call to evaluate these risks without measuring them \cite{bonagiri2026cognitive}. None of these works measures a re-identification rate from twin outputs. That is the gap we fill.

\subsection{Closest prior art and contradictors}

Ward et al. test 13 attacks over 9 generators and 48 tabular datasets, and state in prose a relation between quality and leakage close to ours \cite{ward2025synthmia}; Byun et al. observe a near-monotone frontier of the same type \cite{byun2025riskcontext}. Three differences separate them from our claim: their quality is \textit{aggregate} (MMD, Jensen-Shannon divergence, downstream AUC), never a correspondence to the right person; their risk is \textit{membership} in a training set, not 1-in-N identification; and they publish no coefficient.

Three contradictors hold that fidelity and risk separate: Platzer and Reutterer \cite{platzer2021holdout}; Adams et al., for whom a generator without formal guarantees preserves fidelity and utility without evident privacy harm \cite{adams2025fidelity}; and Shafieinejad et al., observing decoupling as quality saturates within a single tabular diffusion model trained longer \cite{shafieinejad2026diffusion}. Our response is not that they are wrong: all three measure \textit{aggregate} fidelity, never individual correspondence to the right person, and none tests whether the coupling they observe survives a null matched on per-person accuracy. Ours does not (\S\,5.1). Their decoupling also unfolds over training time within one model, where ours is cross-sectional across twelve heterogeneous methods against 1-in-N re-identification.

\section{Threat Models}

We distinguish three scenarios, assuming the first one's strongest objection rather than deflecting it.

\subsection{T1 --- Panel holder publishes unkeyed twins; attacker holds the real answers}

A panel holder releases per-person twin records without the key linking each twin to its respondent. An attacker who holds \textbf{the real answers to the same items} --- an insider, a panel co-owner, a leak of the source file --- matches twins to respondents. This is a \textit{linkability} result in the sense of the Article 29 Working Party and the GDPR, not identification from public information: the quasi-identifier required is a specific questionnaire, rarely held outside the panel.

We state plainly what this is not. It does not show that a third party recovers a named person from public information. And on Twin-2K-500 itself the matching is trivial without any attack --- \texttt{pid} equals \texttt{TWIN\_\allowbreak{}ID}. The result therefore documents a generic risk for anyone releasing ``anonymous'' twins without that key, a practice Twin-2K-500 does not follow. The objection ``this is not real re-identification, the attacker already holds the true answers'' is assumed, not rebutted; our answers are the explicit model above and the open-world measurement of \S\,5.3.

\subsection{T2 --- Two organisations publish twins of the same cohort; attacker holds nothing real}

If several organisations each publish their own twins of the same panel with sufficient item overlap, a third party cross-references them holding no human answer at all. This threat model is named as an open problem by Jordon et al. \cite{jordon2022synthetic} and covered by the A29WP linkability definition, but never measured: Anonymeter's linkability presumes real attribute values in hand \cite{giomi2023unified}, and Guépin et al. target membership in a single generator's training set, never matching between two independent generations \cite{guepin2023synthetic}.

\textbf{We tried to test it directly, and could not}: \S\,5.4's twin-to-twin measurement is same-team and same-persona-file, and the arm built as T2 actually describes fails the interpretability control of \S\,4.5. \textbf{T2 is neither supported nor refuted: it was not tested} (\S\,5.4).

\subsection{T3 --- Pre-publication self-audit}

A panel holder runs the attack against their own file before publication, to decide whether a defense is required --- the constructive use of the method, and the one the artifact (\osname{}) serves.

\subsection{Attacker capabilities, all models}

The attacker performs no training on the target population, has no query access to the generating model, and uses only rank-based matching over categorical vectors.

\section{Data and Methods}

\subsection{Datasets}

\textbf{Twin-2K-500} \cite{toubia2025twin2k500}, 2,058 respondents, public under CC BY 4.0. Target: wave 4, 60 items usable in common out of 108 (40 purchase items of the form ``would you buy this product at this price?'', 20 opinion items). Attacker context where applicable: 494 context items from waves 1--3 plus 14 demographic variables. Chance top-1 in the closed pool is 0.049~\%. A human test-retest condition gives the empirical ceiling: 81.6~\% [79.9~;~83.2] top-1, accuracy 0.745. \textbf{We generated none of these twins.} Every Twin-2K-500 arm we attack, the headline \texttt{JSON Persona -\allowbreak{} GPT4\allowbreak{}.1} included, is taken from that release as published; our repository holds neither the prompt nor the generating code. \textbf{The exact generation parameters of the headline arm are not published by its authors}: the upstream repository releases thirteen sets of outputs but a single configuration file, and that file describes a different arm --- a \texttt{gpt-\allowbreak{}4\allowbreak{}.1-\allowbreak{}mini} model, a persona rendered as \textit{text} rather than JSON, at temperature 0.0. \textbf{We therefore state no model setting, temperature or prompt for the arm this paper reports}, and \S\,7.4 draws the consequence.

\textbf{Park et al. archive} \cite{park2024agents}, 1,052 participants, 177 common items, replication package publicly posted. Of its five agent conditions we analyse four --- composite, interview-only, survey-only, demographic --- the \texttt{persona} condition being excluded by the interpretability control (\S\,4.5). Human retest ceiling 96.8~\%.

\textbf{Argyle et al. archive} \cite{argyle2023outofone}, Harvard Dataverse, \textbf{CC0 1.0}: 4,270 ANES 2016 respondents, of whom \textbf{2,148} answer all \textbf{12} matched items; chance top-1 is 0.047~\%. Each twin column is a GPT-3 (\texttt{davinci}) prediction produced while holding that item out and supplying the person's eleven other \textbf{true} answers, so these twins are \textit{better} informed than our persona twins, not worse: that confound runs against us. Its three twin versions vary decoding, not epoch, and we never present them as survey waves.

No new data was collected. The attack uses only already-published content.

\subsection{Methods compared}

Twelve heterogeneous methods carry the quality-leakage analysis: 8 LLM twins and 4 statistical reference points (Demographics Only, B1 argmax over 14 demographics, B2 argmax, PMM k=10). Additional comparators appear in \S\,5.2: logistic regression on context, a k=1 donor on context, two individualised \textit{generators} (sampled LR, Gaussian copula), and an explicitly advantaged k=1 donor fitted directly on the target block. Sequential CART and a 300-tree forest were tried before that donor and abandoned at 0.45--0.53 accuracy: the retained comparator was not chosen to lose. On the Argyle archive the comparators are \textbf{B-demo}, a leave-one-out imputer on the four demographic items, and \textbf{B-oracle}, the same imputer given the person's eleven other true answers --- the comparator matched to that archive's own conditioning.

\subsection{Metrics}

\textit{Attacks.} Two matching rules are used throughout, and every rate is labelled with the one that produced it. The \textbf{naive} attack ranks candidates by Hamming-type agreement over common items. The \textbf{strong} attack (A-LLR) instantiates the Narayanan--Shmatikov rarity-weighted log-likelihood \cite{narayanan2008robust}, whose parameters we estimate \textbf{out-of-fold over 5 folds}. A third variant (A-MI) scores higher still on the Park archive but estimates its weights \textbf{in sample}, an advantage declared in the preregistration, so we never report it as our result. An \textbf{adaptive} attacker, used only against the defense (\S\,6), additionally knows the mechanism and which items it leaves untouched.

\textit{Closed-world top-1 and top-10}: rank of the true target among all N candidates by the rule stated. \textit{Open-world}: true-positive rate against false-positive rate, where the system may decline to accuse (\S\,5.3). \textit{Bits of identity}: log2 of the ratio of correct one-shot guessing probabilities before and after conditioning on the persona --- a Rényi min-entropy leakage quantity \cite{smith2009foundations}. \textit{Imitation quality}: the share of the human test-retest floor attained.

Unless stated, intervals are 95~\% bootstrap confidence intervals resampling persons, not methods: an interval on a quantity computed across the twelve methods is conditional on those twelve and does not generalise beyond them. Intervals that resample something else --- sub-pool or tie-breaking draws, nulls --- are not confidence intervals on the rate, and are labelled where they appear.

\subsection{Preregistration and self-refutation}

Attack, defense, mechanism, the two archive replications, the disjoint-item test and the distance law were each preregistered according to our records, and we report every refuted prediction in Table~\ref{tab:predictions}. A census found \textbf{no inversion} between a plan and its result across the 28 verifiable pairs; for the large majority the ordering is provable from version-control history, while a small number were committed alongside their results or left unversioned; we state this as a limit rather than claim an unbroken chain. The plans are not yet third-party timestamped. A later audit comparing each prediction's text with its committed plan finds a plan committed strictly before the first result for eleven of Table~\ref{tab:predictions}'s seventeen rows, five committed with or after their results, and one prediction refuted only on a subgroup defined after them (\S\,7.1).

\subsection{Validity controls}

\textit{Provenance.} On all three archives we verified that the attacker's context cannot contain the attacked answers and that no twin copies an earlier wave. Counts, rates and the argument for each archive: the appendix.

\textit{Empty-run validation.} Uniform ranks yield 0.00 bits; the B0 mode baseline yields $-0$.004 [$-0$.003 ; 0.002] bits.

\textit{Interpretability control, adopted after it cost us an experiment.} We state as a rule the control whose absence invalidated two of our own arms (\S\,5.4): \textbf{no experimental arm is interpretable until its candidate pipeline has been shown to beat the demographic baseline in top-1 against the real human answers}, on the pool and items actually attacked, with the baseline recomputed on those same rows rather than carried over as a constant. The check consumes no model call and must precede the first paid contrast; a pipeline that fails it yields twins whose contrasts oppose noise to noise and return chance whatever is manipulated. It has since excluded our own pipeline B, all three GPT-3 twins of the Argyle archive (\S\,5.8), and the Park archive's \texttt{persona} condition (\S\,1.3). Baselines are always taken at the pool size of the arm they judge, being strongly pool-dependent (\S\,5.4); \textbf{and a baseline is only as weak as the attributes it is given} --- the Park archive's is not, in that sense, a demographic baseline at all (\S\,5.8).

\section{Results}

\subsection{The coupling, and the null that absorbs it}

Across 12 heterogeneous methods, the ordering by imitation quality and the ordering by re-identification rate nearly coincide. Three rank correlations appear for that statement, and they are three measurement plans, not three estimates of one number: raw accuracy on whole items, the preregistered disjoint-item test over 50 stratified A/B splits of the 60 items --- \textbf{0.969} [0.937~;~0.993], 100~\% of splits above 0.7, \textbf{prediction (a) confirmed}, the relation is not an artifact of recycled computation --- and \textbf{0.965} on Figure 2's axis (\texttt{fidelite\_\allowbreak{}plancher}), the value the null below is compared against. All these intervals resample \textbf{people within each configuration} and hold the 12 configurations fixed; they are conditional on this set of methods and do not generalise. Resampling the \textbf{configurations} --- the unit the claim is about --- widens the interval substantially without changing its sign, and a permutation test rejects independence; these witnesses are post-hoc, and are reported in full in the appendix.

Prediction (b) was refuted, and the witness that refutes it has since been rebuilt to remove a real defect without changing the verdict: its \textit{wrong} cells avoided the person's true answer (\texttt{c7\_\allowbreak{}disjoint\allowbreak{}.py} l. 133), inflating its leakage by a factor 1.3 (41.2~\% against 31.6~\% once wrong values are drawn from the population marginal). Corrected, it still reproduces the coupling --- \textbf{rho 0.980, 5th--95th percentiles [0.951~;~0.993], against 0.965 observed} over the 12 configurations, 100 replicates as preregistered (Figure 2) --- so \textbf{prediction (b) remains refuted}. It is not an information-free object, and we do not describe it as one: at matched accuracy it leaks \textbf{more} than our twin (31.6~\% top-1 against 20.7~\%).

We therefore do not claim, anywhere in this paper, that a twin's fidelity to its person causes its leakage, nor that fidelity and identifiability are one axis, nor that at equal accuracy every twin leaks more than every predictor: the residuals of the regression on accuracy do \textbf{not} separate the families, 3 of 7 LLM configurations falling below the line. One positive point remains testable outside this circularity: the human retest dominates the best twin on both axes --- 0.303 against 0.154 and 0.520 against 0.067 on the disjoint-split scale, 81.6~\% against 20.7~\% of closed-world top-1 on Figure 2's --- so there is no saturation at the top of the range. The two scales are not interchangeable and nothing here mixes them.

\textit{Limits.} Raw accuracy is only a noisy proxy (r = 0.72, p = 0.008) for the right axis, and Spearman is coarse at n = 12 --- the null touches 1.000 through small-sample effects --- so the verdict concerns the order of magnitude, not the third decimal.

\subsection{Closed-world rates and the comparator question}

At comparable marginal accuracy, no non-LLM predictor we tested comes near the twin: the strongest of them reaches \textbf{0.45~\%} top-1 (the persona-conditioned comparator of the next paragraph) where the twin reaches 20.7~\% [19.0~;~22.4] under the naive attack and \textbf{23.23~\% [21.5~;~25.0]} under the strong one --- \textbf{in a closed world, with the target always present in a pool of 2,058}. The comparator figures are naive-attack rates, which makes the contrast conservative. JSON Persona GPT4.1 attains that rate at accuracy 0.590; the six comparators, at comparable accuracy, all sit at or below \textbf{0.25~\%} top-1 --- among them two individualised \textit{generators}, which answers the objection that we oppose an individualised generator to predictors that regress to the mean. Each rate with its interval: the appendix.

\textbf{At strictly equal input information the gap holds, and that is the form of the claim we defend.} Given the 634 wave-1--3 columns the persona is made of --- from which the 60 attacked items are absent --- the best classical generator conditioned on the individual reaches \textbf{0.45~\%} [0.20~;~0.76] against \textbf{20.66~\%} [19.0~;~22.3] for the LLM twin, both terms from one run. A twin conditioned on the \textbf{demographic segment alone}, not on her past answers, still reaches \textbf{2.15~\%} [1.57~;~2.77] --- \textbf{4.7 times} that best classical comparator, itself fed the individual. The gap is therefore not attributable to the input information. \textit{(Post-hoc, not preregistered.)}

\textbf{Is any of it individual? Three levels, one run.} The standing objection is that a realistic answer vector identifies by its structure alone. A \textbf{real human} answer vector borrowed from another person --- realistic inter-item dependence, no information about the target --- re-identifies 0.000~\% of people and does not separate its target from another member of the same segment (AUC 0.4995 [0.4868~;~0.5124], where 0.5 is no trace); forcing it into the \textbf{right} segment takes it only to 0.146~\% [0.000~;~0.340], an interval still containing zero. The twin reaches 20.668~\% [19.045~;~22.376] in that same run and keeps its advantage \textbf{with the segment held constant}: AUC 0.9076 [0.8990~;~0.9150]. Crediting the non-individual level with the whole 2.15~\% --- too generous, those \textbf{fourteen} demographic questions taking 2055 distinct joint values across the panel, a quasi-identifier and not a group --- the person's share of the rate is 89.6~\%; on the bits-of-identity scale the same attribution gives 37.4~\%, the non-individual level there weighing \textit{more} than the person. \textbf{We publish both, because a share of this kind is meaningless without its scale.} \textit{(Post-hoc, not preregistered.)}

We then gave a comparator an explicit advantage: a k=1 nearest-neighbour donor fitted \textbf{directly on the target block's answers}, seeing what the LLM twin never sees. It carries genuine individual signal and, despite that, obtains a top-1 of \textbf{0.00~\% [0~;~0.18]} (Clopper-Pearson, n = 2,058). \textbf{This claim does not generalise to every leakage metric, and top-10 runs the other way}: that same advantaged comparator reaches a top-10 of \textbf{53.2~\% [51.0~;~55.3]}, \textit{above} the twin's 42.7~\%, so the direction of the whole contrast depends on the choice of k, which no principle fixes at 1. We claim neither that all individualised prediction identifies nor strict accuracy equality --- the generators plateau at 0.476. Neither synthpop/CART nor CTGAN was tested. The donor's accuracy and its within-segment permutation witness: the appendix.

\subsection{Open world --- the defensible measurement}

Removing the closed-world assumption gives the number we consider hardest to attack in review. At a false-accusation rate of 1~\%, the strong attack (A-LLR, parameters estimated out-of-fold over 5 folds) recovers the right person \textbf{4.28~\% [3.3~;~5.6]} of the time on Twin-2K-500, the dataset that carries the claim, and \textbf{60.17~\% [54.5~;~64.4]} on the Park archive, under a person-level bootstrap that \textbf{re-estimates} its parameters in each resample, every copy of a person staying in one fold. At FPR = 0.1~\% the quantity is \textbf{not identified by the data} --- the threshold there rests on one or two absolute false positives, and hundreds of admissible thresholds span almost the whole range of attainable detection rates --- so \textbf{we report no point estimate at that FPR}. At FPR = 1~\% the same construction is stable, and \textbf{the headline figures are the ones at 1~\% FPR}; the threshold census establishing both statements is in the appendix. The human ceiling at FPR = 1~\% is 54.5~\% [50.8~;~57.5] on Twin and 90.7~\% [88.2~;~93.3] on Park.

\textbf{Statistical comparators stay indistinguishable from noise at both FPRs on Twin-2K-500 --- and only there.} Replayed under the same strong attack as the target, Twin's demographic comparator gives 0.00~\% TPR at 0.1~\% FPR and \textbf{0.0486~\%}, one person, at 1~\% FPR, below its own naive value, so the 4.28~\% stands clear of it; PMM k=10 stays at 0.00~\% on both archives under both attacks. On the Park archive the demographic comparator is \textbf{not} at noise once armed with the same attack --- 29.09~\% at 1~\% FPR --- which is why that archive no longer carries this paper's claim (\S\,5.8). These witnesses are post-hoc.

\textbf{These figures replace the ones we first published, and the correction runs in both directions.} The naive attack gave 3.04~\% and 20.39~\% at 1~\% FPR. On Park the strengthening is statistically clear at that FPR --- no overlap, the rate \textbf{triples}. \textbf{On Twin-2K-500 the apparent gain does not survive its interval}: 3.04 $\rightarrow$ 4.28~\% overlaps broadly, so we do not present the strong attacker as identifying better than the naive one here.

\textbf{Our preregistered prediction was refuted, and one half of it then reversed by a better attack} (> 5~\% Twin, > 30~\% Park; Table~\ref{tab:predictions}, row 2).

\subsection{The twin-to-twin channel within one pipeline, what governs it, and why our T2 test does not count}

Two twins of the same person designate each other when they share enough items, with no real data on the attacker's side. The measurements below share one team and, for Twin-2K-500, one persona source per person: they gauge the channel inside a pipeline, not T2 (\S\,3).

\textbf{Twin-2K-500, tabulated by common-item count}. At 60 common items the channel is large and its size badly determined: \textbf{six} configurations give \textbf{30} ordered pairs running from \textbf{11.9~\%} to \textbf{83.6~\%} top-1, mean \textbf{36.4~\%}, 95~\% CI \textbf{[24.7~;~56.5]} clustered by configuration --- the independent unit is the configuration, not the pair, as elsewhere here, so the interval rests on \textbf{six} units and spans a factor above two. \textbf{We never give that mean without its range and its six units, and never as a typical value.} 12 pairs sharing 19 common items: mean top-1 \textbf{0.45~\%}. The anti-artifact control, a decoy from the same segment, stays at or below \textbf{0.10~\%} on both item regimes (the appendix).

\textbf{Park archive replication}: 1,052 agents, 177 common items, top-1 \textbf{11.9~\% [10.1~;~13.8]} and \textbf{13.0~\%} in the two directions, against a decoy control \textbf{at most 0.31~\%}. \textbf{Preregistered prediction refuted}: we set a threshold of at least 20~\% top-1 there and 11.9--13.0~\% does not reach it, though the preregistered \textit{failure} criterion is not met either (the appendix).

\textbf{We never report a single figure for this channel without its common-item count}; a previously circulated average of 26.11~\% pooled the two item regimes and is withdrawn. Nor do we claim leakage crosses waves: that arm is infeasible, with 0 items in common.

\textbf{The channel is not an artifact of a shared prompt template or of demographics.} Decomposing agreement between two twins of one person over the 60 common items, the person contributes \textbf{82~\%} of the rise above the floor between \textit{strangers}, the demographic-ideological segment the rest; a pure-population witness identifies the right person \textbf{0.07~\%} of the time (the appendix).

\textbf{The cross-dataset anomaly, explained in part.} Park shares three times Twin's items and leaks roughly three times \textit{less}, and neither item count nor pool size accounts for it: the 177 GSS items are strongly redundant, and shrinking Twin's pool to Park's \textit{raises} its top-1 rather than lowering it. \textbf{A residue of roughly 3$\times$ remains unexplained}, the remaining candidates --- persona format and length, and the generating pipelines --- not testable on what we hold. The same denominator places the third dataset far below both (\S\,5.8; the appendix).

\textit{Limits.} The 12 low pairs are exactly those at 19 common items, so the effect follows the shared-item count rather than an aberrant configuration. On Park the item-rate relation does not follow Twin's.

\textbf{T2 itself: two genuinely independent pipelines, and a test that does not count.} Two pipelines sharing nothing but the target person --- JSON dossiers against narrative biographies, distinct models --- were matched on the same 60 items (n = 142, stopped by a transport error), giving top-1 1.76~\% [0.35~;~3.63] against a demographic baseline of 9.2~\%. \textbf{We no longer read this as a refutation.} Neither arm produces twins that identify the real person better than chance on that pool, where the Twin team's own twins reach 20.2--38.9~\% (the appendix), so the comparison opposes two noise sources: it measures our instrument, not the world. The 36.4~\% above, a same-pipeline measurement, is not evidence for T2 either, so T2 stands open both ways.

\textbf{Which element differs, not how many --- and a preregistered law our own measurements refute.} Among the six Twin-2K-500 configurations that pass the interpretability control, changing a single published pipeline descriptor gives \textbf{81.4~\%} for decoding alone, \textbf{67.1~\%} for the prompt template, \textbf{33.5~\%} for reasoning and \textbf{17.2~\%} for the model. We preregistered a \textit{distance law} --- leakage falling with the number of published descriptors that differ --- and our own measurements refute it: Spearman \textbf{$-0$.232} [$-0$.321 ; $-0$.187] over 15 pairs, \textbf{+0.003} [$-0$.047 ; +0.068] once the two twins' individual fidelity is controlled, against the $-0$.7 predicted. At a distance of one, rates span 17~\% to 81~\%: the spread \textit{within} a level dwarfs the spread \textit{between} levels. Counting changed components predicts nothing useful; that \textit{naming} them would, four one-off contrasts cannot establish. On Park the same control leaves three pairs, so no replication is possible. Two limits bound this verdict --- arbitrary distance weights, and a small number of independent configurations --- and are stated in the appendix.

\textbf{One-factor-at-a-time from our own pipeline is arithmetically sound but not interpretable.} Pipeline B and all three conditions derived from it identify the real person at chance, so each contrast opposes two noise sources: we withdraw both the reading we first gave those rates --- that changing any single component collapses the channel --- and the refutation of the matching preregistered prediction.

\subsection{Mechanism: being right while deviating --- and three refuted hypotheses}

What identifies is neither the frequency of departures from the modal answer nor answer diversity, but their \textbf{correctness} --- and the whole response vector is required. Correctness of deviations ranks the four objects exactly as leakage does, from 68.5~\% for the human retest down to 39.1~\% for PMM, which deviates and diversifies as much as the twin without leaking. The four values with their leakage rates: the appendix.

The ablation is the strongest evidence: permuting the order of each twin's 40 purchase answers drops closed-world top-1 from \textbf{33.1~\% [31.2~;~35.2] to 0.046~\% [0~;~0.11]}, below chance, and an oracle given only the count of ``yes'' answers gives 0.29~\%. What identifies is the dependence structure between answers, not any answer in isolation. That ablation starts above this paper's headline 20.7~\% because it attacks the \textbf{40 purchase items alone} where the headline uses all \textbf{60}: the 20 opinion items identify 46$\times$ less and dilute a naive Hamming rule rather than help it. That is a change of attack surface, not a discrepancy.

\textbf{Three preregistered hypotheses refuted} --- H1 (per-item entropy drives identification), H4 (twins are more stereotyped than humans) and ``deviations alone carry $\geq$ 80~\% of the leakage'': Table~\ref{tab:predictions}, rows 5--7, with each comparison and its interval in the appendix. We do not present ``wrong deviations alone = 0.0~\%'' as a result: it is a floor of the method.

\textbf{The effect is concentrated, and three convenient explanations are now excluded.} The gap is carried entirely by the 40 purchase items: the twin re-identifies \textbf{33.2~\%} [31.3~;~35.1] of people there, against \textbf{0.08~\%} [0.00~;~0.20] for the best classical comparator explicitly fed the persona columns bearing on consumption and price; on the 20 heuristics-and-biases items the twin (0.24~\%) does not exceed \textbf{that same persona-conditioned classical generator} (0.51~\%) --- against the demographic baseline recomputed on those same 20 columns (0.00~\%) it is above, and \textbf{the two statements name different comparators}. This concentration is \textbf{not a content effect} --- the persona names none of the 40 brands and none of the 40 products, and overlaps the purchase block's vocabulary \textit{less} than the heuristics block's --- and it is \textbf{not explained by instability of the heuristics items}, which remain reliable in humans: normalised by the top-1 a human answering the same items twice achieves, the twin reaches \textbf{44~\%} of that rate on the purchase items and \textbf{1.5~\%} of it on the heuristics items. \textbf{That pair is a ratio between two re-identification rates, not a decomposition of identity information}: it shows the block gap survives dividing by the human ceiling, and names no quantity the twin carries --- the mechanism below stays a named residue. Token overlaps and test-retest kappas: the appendix.

\textbf{The mechanism is open, but bounded --- six explanations are excluded by matched tests rather than by argument.} Item family and modality count cover almost the same partition of the 60 items (Cramér's V 0.96), so we separated them by manipulation. Dichotomised to two modalities, the 20 heuristics items re-identify 0.16~\% [0.02~;~0.33], against 11.37~\% for 20 purchase items matched on item count, modality count \textbf{and} entropy; symmetrically, the 40 purchase items recomposed into 20 four-modality items, at exactly preserved information, still reach 27.3~\%. Modality count therefore has a real but second-order effect, running the wrong way to explain anything. \textbf{The six excluded}: semantic contamination of the persona, leakage of the spending level alone, response noise in the heuristics items, modality count, per-item entropy, and position in the questionnaire (11.0~\% for purchase items matched on position). What stays open is the wording length, the test-retest kappa, and the content of the questions; we propose no mechanism, and we do not offer the item family as one --- it is the name of the residue. \textit{(Post-hoc, not preregistered.)}

\textit{Limits.} The mechanism is established on Twin's purchase block alone and is in apparent tension with the Park archive, where opinion items identify most, \textbf{at lower per-item entropy} (the appendix). What remains is the block effect, the \textbf{best-supported hypothesis, not a result}.

\subsection{Bits of identity: the instrument travels between our two datasets, the rate does not}

An LLM twin costs about 0.4 bits of identity per point of accuracy gained, against 0.068 for the best useful statistical comparator: Twin JSON 4.1 carries \textbf{3.55 bits [3.39~;~3.72]} of a log2 N = 11.01 ceiling and the Park composite \textbf{7.47 [7.27~;~7.66]} of 10.04 (the other objects: the appendix). Transportability is the point. Bits normalised by human entropy give 0.0439 (Twin) against 0.0327 (Park) --- a factor of \textbf{1.34} --- where top-1 varies by a factor of \textbf{3.2}. Demographics Only and PMM leak with \textbf{no accuracy gain at all}, a case a ratio alone would mask. The instrument's validity is bounded on the other side by the third dataset, where a twin carrying no demonstrable individual information has no ratio worth transporting (\S\,5.8).

\textbf{A nuance against our own hypothesis.} The human retest has a ratio of 0.37 (Twin) and 0.44 (Park), as good as or better than the twins: the excess cost exists relative to statistical predictors, \textbf{not relative to a human answering twice}.

\textit{Limits.} The bits are a \textbf{lower bound} (dyadic bound plus Miller-Madow). The sum of per-item mutual information is unusable as a total, as preregistered (the appendix).

\subsection{The risk depends on the recipe --- and we could not reproduce it}

Our own twins, produced in a single call to cheap models from a raw profile, identify almost nobody: 0.00~\% to \textbf{0.83~\% [0~;~2.15]} across 7 cells, against 2.13~\% for the other team's Demographics Only. That interval \textbf{contains} 2.13~\%, so no precise factor is established --- only the order of magnitude, that no model reaches 1~\%. Any previously circulated ``25$\times$ below'' figure is withdrawn. Memorization is not the explanation: llama31-8b, with a training cutoff preceding publication, copies nothing verbatim and leaks no more than the models that do copy --- a control on our own twins, in a regime where none identifies almost anyone, which we do not extend to the published twins of \S\,5.2--\S\,5.4 (\S\,1.3).

\textbf{Two further preregistered predictions refuted} (Table~\ref{tab:predictions}, rows 8--9): R1 $\geq$ 10~\% on at least 2 of 3 models, where no model reaches 1~\%; and call granularity as the explanation, which is not confirmed and is underpowered, with top-10 running \textbf{opposite} to the prediction. We claim neither that leakage comes from the call format nor that the cause is identified. \textbf{It is not.}

\textbf{We then paid to test the obvious explanation, and it failed.} \texttt{openai/\allowbreak{}gpt-\allowbreak{}4\allowbreak{}.1} (Twin's own model class) on Twin's per-item recipe without modification, 30 persons: accuracy \textbf{0.4722 [0.4361~;~0.5050]} against Twin's 0.574; fidelity \textbf{0.1714} against their 0.708; top-1 and top-10 \textbf{0.00~\% [0~;~11.57]} (Clopper-Pearson, n = 30). \textbf{One further preregistered prediction refuted}: accuracy > 0.55; top-1 > 5~\% is \textbf{not refuted}, its interval containing the threshold --- inconclusive for lack of power. We did not make the leakage come back because we did not reach Twin's fidelity, and it is that gap, not the underpowered top-1, that carries the conclusion: at the fidelity reached, the regression over the 12 points of \texttt{c7-\allowbreak{}compromis\allowbreak{}.csv} predicts \textbf{1.10~\%} [$-9$.15 ; 11.35], and the observed 0.00~\% falls inside it.

\textbf{The unknown narrows instead of disappearing, and our failure to reproduce is explained rather than informative.} Neither the model nor the call granularity: what remains implicated is the \textbf{format and length of the profile}. Twin's full JSON persona runs to roughly 121,000 characters --- about 44,348 input tokens, sent whole in a single call --- against our \textbf{8,000-character truncation}, a ratio above fifteen in the information supplied. \textbf{Our locally regenerated arms were therefore never an attempt to reproduce the upstream pipeline; they were a different experiment}, with their own prompts, three distinct open models and a plain-text output. Their failing the interpretability control says nothing about the reproducibility of the published arm, and \textbf{we do not present it as a property of the procedure.}

\textit{Limits.} n = 40 persons for the recipe arm, truncated to n = 10 on the per-item arm, and n = 30 on the paid frontier-model arm; a floor effect is not excluded. Transport failures and retries: Open Science.

\subsection{A second dataset, and a third at the other end of the trajectory}

On the Park archive, the composite agent designates the right participant among 1,052 \textbf{in a closed world} in \textbf{90.40~\% [88.6~;~92.2]} of cases under the strong attack, top-10 97.8~\%, against a human retest ceiling of 96.8~\%. \textbf{This replaces the 65.51~\% [62.7~;~68.3] we first published}: a naive agreement attack understated this archive's leakage by 38~\% in relative terms. An in-sample information-weighted variant reaches 93.25~\%, which we do not claim (\S\,4.3).

\textbf{These Park figures are not evidence that the LLM twin leaks beyond demographics.} Armed with the same rarity-weighted likelihood attacker on the same basin, the demographics-only comparator reaches \textbf{85.17~\%} [82.89~;~87.26] closed-world and \textbf{29.09~\%} TPR at 1~\% FPR: a target-to-comparator ratio of \textbf{1.06$\times$} [1.030~;~1.097] closed-world --- a paired gap of \textbf{+5.2 points} [+2.7 ; +8.1], statistically resolved but 27 times smaller than the 29$\times$ obtained under the naive attack --- and \textbf{2.07$\times$} at 1~\% FPR. \section{Consequences for Publication}

Defenses are developed in a companion paper; we state here only what a panel holder needs, and what we withdraw. \textbf{Permuting answers does not protect.} A joint permutation of whole rows within a demographic segment --- the case most favourable to the family of the Post Randomisation Method \cite{gouweleeuw1998pram} --- costs 0.000 points of utility and leaves top-1 against an adaptive attacker at 20.8~\% [19.1~;~22.5], the undefended level: a permutation hides the index, never the content. Our item-wise variant, D4, stays at 0.24~\% under the strong attack recalibrated on its outputs and at 0.29~\% [0.10~;~0.53] against an adaptive attacker, but it offers \textbf{no formal guarantee}: it republishes each item's within-segment histogram exactly (100.0~\% of segment $\times$ item pairs), so its zero distribution and group costs are that republication, not a cost avoided, and we withdraw the summary that gave its cost as a single mean of 1.47 points. \textbf{Aggregating by the mode over k people of the same segment protects, with a bound}: adaptive top-1 falls to 0.52~\% [0.27~;~0.83] at k = 5 and 0.22~\% [0.07~;~0.40] at k = 10, under top-1 $\leq$ 1/k against any attacker --- a bound on re-identification, not on privacy, since the published mode is itself disclosed. The price is 13.2 and 19.8 points of error on between-segment differences, where the whole gap between the twin and the humans on that component is 2.17 points. \textbf{We withdraw the comparison with differential privacy that we preregistered and first reported} (Table~\ref{tab:predictions}, row 14): our reference generator was fitted on the humans but scored against the twin, and we claim no superiority of any mechanism over differential privacy, in either direction.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

\subsection{The seventeen predictions of Table~\ref{tab:predictions}, and what their precedence shows}

Table~\ref{tab:predictions} lists seventeen predictions; no third-party timestamp exists for any of them. For eleven (nine refuted, both inconclusive), git history shows the preregistration committed strictly before the first result; five (rows 1, 12, 13, 14, 17) were written before computation according to our records but committed with, or after the on-disk existence of, their results. \textbf{Thirteen are recorded as refuted, and only nine of those refutations rest on a precedence git history shows}: rows 1, 12 and 13 are among the five above, and \textbf{row 4 is not a preregistered refutation} --- the preregistered prediction was confirmed, and the refuted subgroup was defined after the results. Table~\ref{tab:predictions}, in the appendix, gives each as written with its verdict, source and precedence. Eleven are also stated where they were measured (\S\,5.1, \S\,5.3, \S\,5.4, \S\,5.5, \S\,5.7, \S\,6), so this count reads without the table. The other two belong here: \textbf{per-item entropy does not rank items with a consistent sign across the two datasets}, and \textbf{the cost per unit of individual fidelity, predicted roughly constant, is not} (\S\,7.2).

\textbf{Two of the thirteen went in our favour, and we mark them rather than bank them}: a stronger attacker gains less than the 20~\% relative we predicted on Twin-2K-500, and an adaptive attacker knowing the mechanism fails to break the defense; both (rows 12 and 13) are among those whose precedence git history does not show. A prediction refuted in the direction one hoped for is worth less than one refuted against.

\textbf{Four verdicts left the count after they were written, each costing us a refutation.} That call granularity explains the leakage, and that the frontier twin identifies more than 5~\% of its cohort, were first recorded as refuted on zero-event samples, where the exact intervals contain the thresholds they were said to exclude: \textbf{inconclusive and underpowered} (\S\,5.7). That two organisations' independent pipelines leak into one another is arithmetically answered (\S\,5.4), but twins on both arms identify the real person at chance, so the contrast decides nothing: \textbf{not testable, counted as neither.} That our defense dominates differential privacy at a moderate budget is \textbf{withdrawn, not decided} --- the comparison behind it was retracted (\S\,6).

\textbf{The census: thirteen recorded refutations --- nine with precedence shown, three without, one on a post-hoc subgroup --- one withdrawn, two inconclusive, one untestable.} Three further outcomes were partial and count as neither (\S\,5.7, \S\,6); one held outright, P2 (\S\,6); three verdicts reached after it closed are reported in place (\S\,5.4, \S\,5.8).

\subsection{What we do not know}

\textbf{An excess cost in bits per unit of fidelity, real but unexplained} (the appendix). With so few points and no mechanism, it \textbf{must not be attributed to ``LLMs'' as a family}.

\textbf{We did not reproduce the leakage with our own twins} (\S\,5.7), including with a paid frontier model on Twin's own per-item recipe --- an open limit, never a mechanism, and a narrower one than before: neither the model nor the call granularity, leaving the persona's format and length. \textbf{Nor can we reproduce the published arm itself}, whose generation parameters its authors have not released (\S\,4.1, \S\,7.4).

\textbf{Whether the twin-to-twin channel crosses independent pipelines.} Everything we report on it is measured inside a shared pipeline, and the arm built to decide the cross-pipeline case fails the control of \S\,4.5: \textbf{T2 is untested, not refuted} (\S\,5.4).

\textbf{Why the effect concentrates on one block.} On Twin-2K-500 the whole gap lives in the 40 purchase items. \textbf{The mechanism is open, and we offer no candidate --- but it is bounded}: six explanations, semantic contamination and modality count among them, are excluded by matched tests (\S\,5.5).

\textbf{Rates do not predict across datasets.} The normal-maxima model lands close on Park k = 60 but fails both its controls (the appendix): an isolated success between two failures is a coincidence, not a law. Only conditional information retains sign and order of magnitude across datasets (r = 0.72 and 0.89). The twin-to-twin channel's cross-dataset rate is likewise only partly explained, a residue of roughly 3$\times$ surviving both controls (\S\,5.4). And nothing we hold separates the trajectory of \S\,5.8 from the protocols that differ along it (\S\,1.1).

\subsection{Multiplicity}

Most of this paper's central results are bootstrap confidence intervals around a descriptive measure --- re-identification rate, rank correlation, bits of identity --- not classical hypothesis tests; of the 34 \textbf{adjudicated tests} underpinning those claims at the time of the census (not the 17 preregistered predictions --- the appendix sets the three counts side by side), 11 were refuted and 3 judged inconclusive, roughly one third, the opposite of the signature of data dredging. \textbf{That rate controls the auxiliary family, not the headline}: none of 20.7~\%, 90.40~\% and 60.17~\% is a preregistered threshold --- each is descriptive, chosen among competing metrics. Holm and Benjamini-Hochberg were both applied to the 15 tests convertible to a p-value. \textbf{Neither changes any verdict}, and the two agree on all 15: the p-values are bimodal, so no claim here rests on a marginally significant result. The intervals carrying the other claims must be read as measurements, each with its own margin, not as independent rejections of a common null. The adjusted p-values, the full 47-test census across three families, and why it and Table~\ref{tab:predictions} do not carry the same totals: the appendix.

Three thresholds are fragile, and we name them rather than let a reviewer find them: the frontier twin's \textbf{fidelity > 0.10} bar, confirmed at 0.1714 but refuted had it been set at 0.18 --- while our own weakest known twin sits at 0.177; the choice of k in top-k (\S\,5.2); and the functional form fitted to the six scale points, which is why \S\,5.9 extrapolates beyond them to nothing at all. A common bootstrap across analyses reusing the same 2,058 people was abandoned for cost, so the intervals of \S\,5.1--\S\,5.3 on Twin carry no simultaneous coverage.

\subsection{Scope, a single generation, and two limits established by failure}

\textbf{The headline rate rests on one generation, that generation is not ours, and it is not reproducible as things stand.} The twins we attack were produced \textbf{once}, by the upstream team, and \textbf{no replicate of that condition exists}. Worse for us, \textbf{the condition itself is not identified}: the upstream repository publishes thirteen sets of outputs and one configuration file describing a different arm (\S\,4.1), so we can state neither model setting, temperature nor prompt behind the rate we report. \textbf{Re-running the headline arm is therefore not something we can price, only something the upstream prompt would unblock}; a paid pilot stopped there (the appendix). The published interval [18.96~;~22.43] is a bootstrap over \textit{people}. At strictly constant basin, items, attack and tie-breaking convention, the seven twin configurations available at full sample span \textbf{2.13~\%} to \textbf{20.68~\%}, a range of 18.55 points against 3.47 points of interval width, and 99.07~\% of the observed variability is attributable to the \textbf{configuration} against 0.93~\% to the people: our intervals measure that hundredth with care and say nothing about the other ninety-nine. The arm we report is the \textbf{maximum} of the eight measured and also the \textbf{most faithful} of them (accuracy 0.574, rank 1 of 8), which makes that maximum expected rather than suspect; both facts belong together. \textbf{This figure must therefore be read as the rate of the most faithful configuration we hold, not the rate of an LLM twin in general.} We do not claim the generation variability is small: we did not measure it, and the decomposition above is a \textbf{majorant} of it.

Three datasets, one language, one questionnaire format, categorical closed-choice answers only. The mechanism analysis and the defense both rest on a single block of a single dataset, and we make no claim about conversational agents or panels outside the three studied here. Two further limits, each stated with the resource that would lift it.

\textbf{Generality beyond American attitude instruments, and the audit bottleneck.} All three datasets are American survey panels built on neighbouring instrument families, so we cannot say whether this channel is a property of that family --- nor, as \S\,1.1 states, separate the trajectory from the protocols that differ along it. The obstacle is that the matched file --- row-by-row, not by demographic cell --- is almost never redistributed: we verified three such datasets at the source and ran no census, so no upper bound is established, and \textbf{on a typology of four cases and on no rate, the risk appears to be created faster than it becomes auditable.} The four cases, and the resource that would lift this: the appendix.

\textbf{Free-text outputs.} We measure nothing about free text, because \textbf{none of the three archives publishes twin text} (the appendix, with the resource that would lift this). Every rate here concerns closed-choice categorical answers, and we extrapolate in neither direction: the free-text inference literature \cite{staab2024beyond,ko2026weakcues} attacks a richer signal, so our figures are no upper bound there.

\textbf{Mechanism, the three refuted hypotheses and the concentration controls (\S\,5.5).} H1 (entropy): opinion items carry 2.10 bits against 0.99 for purchase items and identify 46$\times$ less --- top-1 0.24~\% [0.07~;~0.46] against 11.0~\% [9.9~;~12.3] for the purchase subset matched to them on entropy, a 20-item comparison. \section{Detail behind the four audits of 13 September 2026}

Each of the four audits below (a fifth, on the defense candidates, moved to the companion paper) is published with its preregistration, its script and its result CSV; this section gives the reasoning and the reservations, and the named files carry the tables.

\textbf{Three levels of signal, one run (\S\,5.2)} --- \texttt{resultats/\allowbreak{}c7-\allowbreak{}decomposition-\allowbreak{}signal\allowbreak{}.csv}. All conditions share basin, items, attack and tie-breaking convention. Top-1 is reported under the repository's convention, random tie-breaking averaged over 20 draws at a fixed seed, while the \textbf{AUC with the segment held constant} counts ties as one half and depends on no draw, no seed and no convention: that is why it, and not top-1, carries the verdict. The Demographics Only twin discriminates even against each person's \textit{nearest demographic neighbour}, who shares eleven of the fourteen answers in median, where all three structure-only floors stay at chance --- which is what makes it a quasi-identifier rather than a segment. Under the more defensible reading of the segment level, the borrowed human vector of the same segment, the two scales agree instead of diverging; we publish the least favourable attribution. \textit{What we could not verify}: the archive publishes this configuration's outputs, not its prompt template, so we cannot assert that the fourteen Demographics questions are exactly those passed to it. The weaker claim suffices --- whatever its prompt, this twin discriminates \textit{inside} both of the repository's segmentations, so its input is strictly finer than a demographic segment.

\textbf{Arming Twin's comparator (\S\,5.8)} --- \texttt{resultats/\allowbreak{}c7-\allowbreak{}audit-\allowbreak{}twin-\allowbreak{}comparateur\allowbreak{}.csv}. The fourteen Demographics questions are QID11--QID24, none of them re-asked in wave 4. Restricted to the eleven attributes homologous to Park's they leave slightly fewer Twin respondents unique than Park's block does; at fourteen they pass it. The mechanism is measurable on the items themselves: Twin's fourteen attributes shift the majority-response share of the attacked items over same-size random groups by about a quarter of the shift Park's eleven produce. For the ratio to fall to the collapse threshold of 2.0$\times$, Twin's comparator would have to reach almost fifteen times the published comparator.

\textbf{Separating item family from modality count (\S\,5.5)} --- \texttt{resultats/\allowbreak{}c7-\allowbreak{}audit-\allowbreak{}modalites-\allowbreak{}familles\allowbreak{}.csv} and \texttt{resultats/\allowbreak{}c7-\allowbreak{}audit-\allowbreak{}contamination\allowbreak{}.csv}. Semantic contamination is excluded by a comparator fed the related persona predictors, which stays near zero where the twin identifies a third of the block and the positive witness of the same apparatus identifies most of it; the weak form --- leakage of the spending level alone --- falls with it, since destroying the \textit{pattern} of a twin's ``yes'' answers while conserving their \textit{count} per person collapses the rate. We do not present these two audits as independent confirmations: they bear on the same columns, 39 items out of 40.

\textbf{Generation variance (\S\,7.4)} --- \texttt{resultats/\allowbreak{}c7-\allowbreak{}variance-\allowbreak{}generation\allowbreak{}.csv}. Of the 13 configurations the upstream team published, 8 pass our provenance audit; 7 of those attack the full basin and enter the variance computation, the eighth attacking a smaller sample and being reported for information only. \textbf{Why that variability is not merely unmeasured but unpriceable (\S\,4.1, \S\,7.4)} --- \texttt{resultats/\allowbreak{}c7-\allowbreak{}pilote-\allowbreak{}regeneration-\allowbreak{}resultats\allowbreak{}.md}. \begin{ethics}

\textbf{Datasets, and the terms we are bound by.} Twin-2K-500 is public under CC BY 4.0 and the Argyle archive under CC0 1.0. The Park replication package is publicly posted on OSF (node \texttt{t6g7k}), but public posting is not a licence: the OSF API returns \texttt{node\_\allowbreak{}license: null} for that node, so \textbf{no licence is declared} and public access carries no right to redistribute the individual responses it contains. Its questionnaire content is a General Social Survey instrument, and \textbf{NORC's terms of use forbid reproducing the GSS in any form without prior written consent}. We record these constraints because an article about privacy that passes over the terms of use of its own data disqualifies itself.

\textit{What we do in consequence, verified rather than assumed.} We redistribute no individual response file from any of the three archives. We checked our own released material file by file: the review artifact (\osname{}) ships only code and a synthetic dataset generated at run time and reads nothing from \texttt{data/\allowbreak{}}, which is excluded from version control; every published table under \texttt{resultats/\allowbreak{}} is an aggregate by condition, by item or by bootstrap replicate; and the Argyle analysis prints and writes no ANES case identifier and no individual match. Only analysis code and disclosure-reviewed aggregates are published, with the NORC citation and attribution to Park et al. Two limits we state rather than resolve: NORC's published terms address reproduction of the GSS and do not speak to derived aggregate statistics, the reading that per-item aggregates fall outside the prohibition being ours and not NORC's; and the ANES clause covering the public variables the Argyle archive carries was not verified at its own source, the Dataverse deposit itself being CC0.

No new data was collected: the attack uses only already-published content (human answers and twin outputs). A limit to note: participants did not specifically consent to a re-identification test on the twins generated from their answers.

\textbf{No individual is identified.} No identifier linking a record to a real person is published or listed; only rates aggregated over the full cohorts are reported.

\textbf{No new harm on Twin-2K-500.} It already publishes, for each person, their identifiers and real answers beside their twin: matching is trivial without any attack. The result documents a generic risk for anyone releasing ``anonymised'' twins without that key --- a practice Twin-2K-500 does not follow.

\textbf{The Park archive is a different case, and we make no such claim there.} That argument rests on a key published beside the twins and does not transfer: no trivial alignment makes the matching free there, the rates are far higher, and the objects we attack are the very ones whose individual release the authors treated as a privacy matter. What limits the harm is narrower: we publish no individual record and no per-person result from that archive, only aggregate rates, and the disclosure below is addressed to its authors, whose own warning this work quantifies.

\textbf{Benefit.} Several teams publish individualised LLM twin outputs without any linkage risk analysis. This provides a first measured figure for that risk on public datasets, and a reproducible method --- attack plus interpretability control --- for testing it \textit{before} publication.

\textbf{Responsible disclosure, and its actual state.} Letters to the Twin-2K-500 authors and to Park et al., with a 30-day response window and an offer to share code and report in advance, were sent on 12 September 2026 in two waves to six research groups in all: at Columbia University, Stanford University and Centerdata, then, later the same day, at the University at Buffalo, the University of Southern California and the Technical University of Munich. The 30-day window opened by the first wave closes on 12 October 2026. The tone toward Park et al. is fixed: \textit{your warning was accurate, here is its measured magnitude}. \textbf{As of 13 September 2026, none of the six groups has replied.} Sending the letters was on the critical path, ahead of any preprint posting or code release. We write this plainly rather than imply a consultation that has produced answers: the committee should know the window was open, and unanswered, when this manuscript was drafted.

\textbf{The Park et al. case.} Their supplementary material already warned participants that their information might be ``inadvertently shared'' and acknowledged that complete anonymity remains challenging. They named the risk, obtained an ethics agreement worked over more than six months, pseudonymised, restricted access to part of the material and planned a 25-year withdrawal. What is nevertheless public is the replication package we analyse, which carries the 1,052 participants' individual responses together with the agent conditions' responses (\S\,4.1): the protection announced in the paper does not cover the objects this attack consumes. We flag that gap as a question for the authors rather than a finding against them --- from outside we cannot tell whether the restriction was lifted or never covered the replication package --- and it is precisely what the disclosure letter must state correctly. No public audit had measured the magnitude of that named risk; our figures (90.40~\% closed-world, 60.17~\% open-world at 1~\% false accusations, and 44.7~\% from the interview alone, a lower bound) are, to our knowledge, its first quantification --- with the qualification established in \S\,5.8, that an identically armed demographics-only comparator reaches 85.17~\% on this archive, so what is quantified is the linkage risk the release carries and not a margin the LLM agent adds --- and our own first measurement understated it.

\textbf{Publishing the attack code.} \textit{For}: replicability, and enabling other panel holders to test their twins before publication. \textit{Against}: it lowers the cost for an attacker already holding a third-party panel's real answers and a corresponding twin file. \textit{Our resolution}: publish, because the code only acts if the attacker already holds real answers to the same items, and the countermeasure is straightforward once the risk is known.

\textbf{Recommended defenses.} (a) Do not publish twin outputs item by item for blocks with high inter-person specificity; publish aggregates or calibrated noise. (b) Break the person-twin alignment in public files. (c) Publish regenerated twins without copying wave metadata (\texttt{StartDate}/\texttt{EndDate}/\texttt{Duration}/\texttt{RecordedDate}). (d) Run a minimal linkage test, and the interpretability control of \S\,4.5, before any twin release: both are free, and the second says whether the first can be interpreted at all. (e) Within-segment shuffling (\S\,6), with its real cost stated: 4.4 points on inter-item correlations --- a single permutation draw, and above the 4.32 points of amplitude there was to destroy, so the component is destroyed in full (\S\,6) --- a 68.1~\% worsening of the gap to human correlations (5.775 $\rightarrow$ 9.709), and the loss of every inter-item analysis downstream (\S\,6) --- and with what it does not provide: \textbf{no formal guarantee}, and exact republication of each item's within-segment histogram, so an adversary who knows a segment's other members recovers the target's answers. It holds at 0.29~\% against an attacker who knows the mechanism, but only for a split that shuffles the informative block (\S\,6). (f) Differential privacy where the published object can be a population statistic rather than a per-person twin; we make no claim about its cost relative to (e), having withdrawn the comparison we preregistered (\S\,6).

\textbf{Public interest.} The AAPOR report of 8 May 2026 \cite{aapor2026responsibleai} ranks synthetic response generation as the most risky of the core tasks it evaluates and names re-identification by linkage without quantifying it. A risk named by professional bodies but never measured justifies an independent measurement followed by responsible disclosure rather than silence.

\textbf{Ethics review and scope.} This study was not submitted to an external ethics panel such as an IRB. It relies exclusively on secondary data already made public by their original publishing teams, and no new data was collected from any person: we surveyed no one, interviewed no one, and had no contact with any data subject. On that basis we judged the study out of scope for human-subjects review, with the limit recorded above --- participants did not specifically consent to a re-identification test on the twins generated from their answers. The scoping does not extend to the released artifact, which processes no real person's data at all (\osname{}).
